How to Combine Multiple Photos into One PDF on iPhone

Combining multiple pictures into a single, cohesive PDF is essential when sending multi-page contracts, utility receipts for tax filings, or school assignments from your phone. Sending several loose image attachments clutters inboxes and risks pages being viewed out of order.

By Quan Dang · November 2026

Why Combine Photos into a Single PDF?

When sharing documentation digitally, presentation and sequencing matter:

  • Guaranteed reading sequence: A single multi-page PDF ensures the recipient views page one, two, and three in the exact chronological order intended.
  • Single attachment simplicity: Rather than emailing ten separate photo files that might get scattered across email threads, you deliver one organized attachment.
  • Universal device compatibility: PDFs render consistently across iPhones, iPads, Macs, Windows PCs, and Android devices without requiring specific photo viewers.

Below are the free built-in methods included with iOS, followed by an on-device app designed for advanced multi-page formatting.

Built-in Method 1: The Files App Multi-Select Route

Apple includes a native PDF compiler inside the Files app. If your photos are already in Files (or if you share them from the Photos app to Files first), you can merge them in seconds. Apple details folder management and file actions in Use the Files app on iPhone.

  1. If your pictures are in the camera roll, open Photos, tap Select, choose your pictures, tap Share, and choose Save to Files.
  2. Open the Files app and navigate to that destination folder.
  3. Tap the More icon (...) in the top right and tap Select.
  4. Tap each image you want to combine. Note that iOS compiles pages according to file name or date modified.
  5. Tap the More icon (...) in the bottom toolbar.
  6. Select Create PDF.

iOS will compile the selected images into a single document named after the first selected image. It is free, instant, and works offline.

Built-in Method 2: Apple Notes Multi-Page Document Scanner

If you have not taken the photos yet and are holding physical sheets of paper, the document scanner in Apple Notes is a seamless built-in option. Apple explains scanning in Notes in How to scan documents on your iPhone or iPad.

  1. Open the Notes app and create a new note.
  2. Tap the Camera icon in the toolbar above your keyboard.
  3. Tap Scan Documents.
  4. Point your iPhone camera at each page in order. The camera automatically detects paper borders and captures each sheet sequentially.
  5. Tap Save when you have captured all pages.
  6. Tap the scanned document thumbnail inside your note, tap the Share button, and tap Save to Files or email the resulting PDF.

This method bundles every scanned page into one clean PDF without requiring any third-party installation.

Built-in Method 3: Apple Shortcuts Automation

For repetitive tasks, Apple allows users to build custom multi-image shortcuts. Learn more about building iOS shortcuts in Apple's official documentation: Shortcuts User Guide for iPhone.

  1. Open the Shortcuts app on your iPhone.
  2. Tap (+) to create a new shortcut.
  3. Add the Select Photos action and toggle on "Select Multiple".
  4. Add the Make PDF action directly below it.
  5. Add a Share action to distribute the finished file.
  6. Save the workflow to your Home Screen.

Whenever you tap the shortcut icon, it prompts you to select photos and immediately converts them into a consolidated PDF file.

Pain Points with Built-in Methods

While Apple's native methods cover simple cases, users dealing with multi-page paperwork frequently encounter practical hurdles:

  • Zero drag-and-drop sequencing: Built-in tools do not provide a visual canvas to drag, drop, and rearrange pages before generating the PDF. If a page is accidentally compiled upside down or in the wrong order, you must restart from scratch.
  • Excessive document file size: Combining ten photos taken with modern iPhone cameras easily yields an 80MB to 120MB PDF. Most email servers immediately bounce attachments larger than 25MB.
  • Irregular page dimensions: Photos shot in portrait and landscape orientations get merged haphazardly, producing inconsistent page borders rather than a uniform US Letter or A4 document.
  • No text layer or redaction: Standard built-in photo merges do not apply on-device OCR to make pages searchable or allow blacking out private financial numbers before sending.
