How to Turn Photos into PDF on iPhone with No Watermark

If you need to submit official paperwork, an insurance claim, a school assignment, or a lease agreement from your iPhone, the last thing you want stamped across your document is a third-party logo or promo banner. Fortunately, you do not need to pay a subscription or tolerate intrusive branding just to create a clean PDF.

Why Watermarks Appear on Mobile Scans

Many scanner utilities on the App Store allow users to scan pages for free but insert large promotional watermarks across the header or diagonal body of exported pages unless an ongoing subscription is purchased. For formal submissions to banks, courts, employers, or government agencies, documents bearing third-party advertising look unprofessional and can even lead to rejection.

To avoid unwanted branding, iOS offers multiple completely free options: built-in Apple system tools that never add watermarks, and free privacy-first utilities like img2pdf that process files locally without branding.

Method 1: Apple's Built-in Document Scanner (Notes & Files)

If you have physical paper documents in front of you, Apple includes a native document scanner directly inside iOS. It uses camera vision to detect paper corners, flatten perspective, correct lighting, and stitch pages into a clean, unwatermarked PDF.

Apple details the step-by-step instructions for this feature in their official guide: How to scan documents on your iPhone or iPad.

To scan directly to a PDF in the Files app:

  1. Open the built-in Files app on your iPhone.
  2. Navigate to the folder where you want to store your document (such as "On My iPhone").
  3. Tap the More button (three dots) in the top-right corner, then choose Scan Documents.
  4. Position your iPhone over the paper. The camera will automatically detect edges and take the capture.
  5. Continue scanning additional pages, then tap Save.

The resulting document is a multi-page PDF saved directly in your file system without any added marks, text stamps, or company logos.

Method 2: Print-to-PDF Gesture in the Photos App

What if the images are already sitting in your iPhone camera roll? Apple provides a hidden print preview gesture that converts existing photos into a PDF without third-party software. Apple explains general iOS printing steps in Print from iPhone.

Here is how to generate a PDF using the print preview trick:

  1. Open the Photos app and tap Select in your library.
  2. Tap the photos you wish to include in your document.
  3. Tap the Share button (box with an upward arrow) in the bottom-left corner.
  4. Scroll down the Share Sheet and select Print.
  5. On the Print Options screen, you will see a preview thumbnail at the bottom. Perform a two-finger pinch-out gesture (as if zooming into a picture) on the preview thumbnail.
  6. The preview expands into a full PDF reader. Tap the Share icon at the top or bottom of this screen to save the PDF to Files or attach it to an email.

This system technique creates a clean PDF without watermarks, though it provides limited control over page margins, reordering, or document file size.

Method 3: Create PDF Action in Apple Files

If your images are saved in the Files app (or you share photos from Photos into Files first), iOS includes a quick action to combine them. Apple outlines folder navigation in Use the Files app on iPhone.

  1. Open Files and navigate to the folder containing your pictures.
  2. Tap the More icon (...) and tap Select.
  3. Select the photos you want to merge.
  4. Tap the More icon (...) in the bottom toolbar and choose Create PDF.

iOS will instantly output a combined PDF containing your photos. It is watermark-free, fast, and completely offline.

When Apple's Built-in Methods Fall Short

While Apple's native utilities are convenient, they were designed for simple tasks rather than flexible document management. Users frequently encounter these practical limitations:

  • No visual page reordering: The Files app combines images according to alphabetical file name or date modified. You cannot drag and drop thumbnails to adjust page order before generating the file.
  • Oversized file sizes: Photos taken with a 48MP iPhone camera are huge. Converting three high-resolution photos with the Print-to-PDF gesture often creates a 25MB to 40MB PDF that email servers reject.
  • Missing page standardisation: Photos converted via print preview often stretch awkwardly across pages or retain odd aspect ratios, with no quick way to fit images onto standard US Letter or A4 sheets.
  • No on-device OCR or redaction: Built-in multi-photo generation does not automatically create an invisible OCR text layer across images, nor does it allow blacking out private financial or medical details.
